What is this environment?

Canary is a vulnerable automotive (in-vehicle) lab built around a small ECU subsystem on a real CAN bus. It models a certification-bench scenario: the sample that an assessor at a TIC company (such as DEKRA) receives for a UNECE R155 and ISO/SAE 21434 evaluation, a representative subsystem with an external service interface and an internal vehicle network. The student plays the assessor.

1. In-vehicle ECUs: two ECUs simulated on the Raspberry Pi over two MCP2515 + TJA1050 CAN nodes sharing one bus: a Central Gateway (CGW) on can0 and a Body Control Module (BCM) on can1.

2. Service interface: a SOME/IP service over Automotive Ethernet (:30509/udp) that fronts the bus. A remote call is translated by the gateway into a CAN frame that drives the actuator.

3. Controlled function: phase 0 wires a single vehicle function end to end, central locking. A SOME/IP SetLock becomes CAN LOCK_CMD (0x120), the BCM actuates and reports LOCK_STAT (0x121).

This is the functional bring-up. There are no intentional vulnerabilities yet, the attack surface (SOME/IP without authentication, CAN injection and replay, UDS diagnostics, unsigned OTA) arrives in later phases. The lab runs with or without the physical modules: when none are attached, a virtual CAN interface simulates the bus so the whole SOME/IP to CAN chain still works.

Where to start?

Click "Load Canary" to deploy the lab on the Raspberry Pi. The init hooks detect the CAN modules (or fall back to simulation), bring the interfaces up, and start the two ECU services.

Then drive it from the PC. The reference SOME/IP client sends lock, unlock, and state over UDP: python3 tools/someip_client.py 192.168.2.1 lock. With a USB-CAN adapter on the bus, candump shows LOCK_CMD 0x120 and LOCK_STAT 0x121.

Detailed launch, protocol maps, and the vulnerability roadmap live in the repo docs: docs/Canary/Canary.md and docs/Canary/Vulns/README.md (OWASP plus R155 / ISO 21434 mapping).

Lab prerequisites

  • Ethernet: a direct cable between Raspberry Pi and PC (Pi 192.168.2.1, PC static 192.168.2.2/24) for the SOME/IP service.
  • CAN modules (optional): 2x MCP2515 + TJA1050 on the Pi's SPI0 (CE0 -> can0, CE1 -> can1) with separate INT GPIOs and a 120 ohm terminator at each of the two bus ends. Without them the lab runs in vcan simulation.
  • USB-CAN adapter (tester): the attacks and tests come from the PC, so the tester clips a USB-CAN adapter onto the bus, the way a bench tester plugs into the OBD-II port.
  • Isolation: run on a dedicated, air-gapped LAN. This is deliberately vulnerable and, with real modules, moves a physical actuator.

Device architecture

  • Central Gateway - CGW (can0): hosts the SOME/IP CentralLockingService (SetLock, GetLockState, LockStatus) on :30509/udp and translates it to the CAN bus.
  • Body Control Module - BCM (can1): listens for LOCK_CMD, actuates the central lock (state file plus an optional GPIO indicator), and transmits LOCK_STAT on change and as a 500 ms heartbeat.
  • CAN bus (can0 / can1, or vcan0): classic CAN at 500 kbit/s, LOCK_CMD 0x120 and LOCK_STAT 0x121.
  • Test model (Model A): the tester works from the PC: Ethernet for the SOME/IP layer, a USB-CAN adapter for the bus. The Pi stays a clean vehicle image with no attacker tooling on board.
